How to Calculate a Weighted Grade: Formula and Examples

Learn the weighted grade formula, calculate category grades by their weights, and avoid common grading mistakes with a worked example.

A weighted grade gives different assignments or course categories different levels of importance. For example, exams may count for 50% of a final grade while homework counts for 20%. Because each category contributes a different share, simply averaging every score can produce the wrong result.

The weighted grade formula

For each category, multiply the grade by its weight expressed as a decimal. Add the weighted values to get the final grade when the weights total 100%. In formula form: weighted grade = Σ(grade × weight). Convert a percentage weight to a decimal by dividing it by 100.

Worked example

Imagine a class where exams are 50% of the grade and you earned 84%; projects are 30% and you earned 92%; homework is 20% and you earned 95%. The calculation is (84 × 0.50) + (92 × 0.30) + (95 × 0.20) = 42 + 27.6 + 19 = 88.6%. Your weighted grade is 88.6%.

Check that weights add up

Before calculating, add all category weights. They should usually total 100%. If they total 90% or 110%, check the syllabus: a missing category or incorrectly entered weight can change the result. If the weights intentionally total something other than 100, normalize them by dividing each weight by the total weight.

Weighted grade vs. simple average

A simple average treats each score equally. A weighted average does not. If your final exam is worth half the course grade, it should affect the result much more than a small homework task. Use the grading policy supplied by your teacher rather than assuming all marks count equally.

Common mistakes

Do not enter 30 instead of 0.30 if your formula expects decimal weights. Do not mix raw points with percentages unless you convert them to a common scale. Also check whether the course uses category weights, total points, dropped assignments, extra credit, or a curve; those policies can affect the official grade.

Frequently asked questions

Can weights add up to more than 100%?

Usually course weights should total 100%. If your syllabus says otherwise, confirm how the instructor expects them to be normalized.

Can I calculate a missing grade?

Yes. If the final target and all other category scores and weights are known, rearrange the weighted-average equation to solve for the missing score.

Note: This guide is educational and explains a general calculation method. Check your own source data and applicable policies before relying on a result.
