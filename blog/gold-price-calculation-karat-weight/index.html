How to Calculate Gold Price by Weight and Karat

Learn how gold purity, weight, and a quoted pure-gold price combine to estimate a piece's metal value before fees or dealer deductions.

Gold value depends on more than the number shown on a scale. You need the item's weight, its purity or karat, and a reference price for pure gold in the same currency and weight unit. The result is an estimate of the metal value—not necessarily the amount a buyer will pay.

Understand karat and purity

24-karat gold is treated as nearly pure gold for common calculations. 18K gold is 18/24, or 75%, gold; 14K is 14/24, or about 58.33%; and 10K is 10/24, or about 41.67%. Actual fineness can vary by hallmark and local standards.

Gold value formula

First convert the item's weight to the same unit used by the reference price. Then multiply weight by purity and by the pure-gold price per unit. For example, if an item contains 10 grams of 18K gold and the reference price is $70 per gram of pure gold, the estimated metal value is 10 × (18/24) × $70 = $525.

Keep units consistent

Gold may be quoted per troy ounce, gram, or another unit. One troy ounce is approximately 31.1035 grams. Do not use an ordinary ounce conversion for precious metals, and do not multiply a per-ounce quote by a weight measured in grams without converting one of them.

Spot price is not a guaranteed offer

The spot price is a market reference and can change throughout the trading day. A jewelry buyer or refiner may deduct costs, apply a margin, or evaluate the item based on recoverable metal. Jewelry design, gemstones, craftsmanship, brand, and collector value may also matter, but a basic melt-value calculation does not capture them.

Check the assumptions before selling

Confirm the hallmark, weigh the item accurately, identify whether stones or non-gold components are included in the weight, and use a current quote. If the item contains mixed karats or several pieces, calculate each portion separately. For a major transaction, compare multiple reputable buyers and ask how their offer is calculated.

Frequently asked questions

Is the calculated amount what a shop will pay?

Not necessarily. It estimates metal value from the inputs; buyers may offer less after refining costs, margins, and their own testing.

Can I compare gold prices in different currencies?

Yes, but convert the reference price and keep weight units consistent before comparing estimates.

Note: This guide is educational and explains a general calculation method. Check your own source data and applicable policies before relying on a result.
