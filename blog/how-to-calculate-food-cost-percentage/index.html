How to Calculate Food Cost Percentage for a Restaurant

Use the food cost percentage formula to price menu items, compare ingredient costs with sales, and spot common calculation errors.

Food cost percentage compares the cost of food ingredients with the sales revenue from those dishes. Restaurants and food businesses use it to monitor menu profitability, but it is only one part of operating costs. Labor, rent, utilities, waste, delivery fees, and other expenses also affect profit.

Food cost percentage formula

For a single dish, divide the ingredient cost per serving by the selling price per serving, then multiply by 100. If a dish costs $3.60 in ingredients and sells for $12, the food cost percentage is ($3.60 ÷ $12) × 100 = 30%.

Calculate ingredient cost per serving

Add the cost of each ingredient used in the recipe, accounting for the amount actually used. If a bulk ingredient costs $8 per kilogram and a recipe uses 150 grams, that ingredient contributes $1.20 before considering waste or yield loss. Divide batch cost by the number of sellable servings to estimate cost per serving.

Use the right selling price

When you know the desired food cost percentage, rearrange the formula: target selling price = ingredient cost per serving ÷ target food cost rate. For $3.60 in ingredients and a 30% target, the calculated price is $3.60 ÷ 0.30 = $12. This is a starting point; market positioning, taxes, fees, and overhead can affect the final menu price.

Recipe cost vs. period food cost

Recipe-level food cost uses ingredient cost for a dish divided by its selling price. A period-level food cost ratio may use beginning inventory + purchases − ending inventory, divided by food sales. These approaches answer different questions, so avoid mixing recipe costs with a period's inventory totals.

Track waste and portion consistency

Portion sizes, spoilage, over-preparation, complimentary items, and inconsistent recipes can increase actual food cost. Update ingredient prices regularly and train staff to follow standardized recipes. Compare theoretical recipe cost with actual inventory-based cost to identify possible waste or recording issues.

Food cost is not profit margin

A 30% food cost means ingredients represent 30% of sales under the chosen calculation. It does not mean the business earns a 70% net profit. The remaining revenue must cover labor and all other operating expenses.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good food cost percentage?

There is no universal target for every restaurant. The right benchmark depends on cuisine, service model, labor costs, location, and pricing strategy.

Should sales tax be included in menu revenue?

Use a consistent accounting definition and follow your local accounting rules; taxes collected for authorities are generally not treated as business revenue.
