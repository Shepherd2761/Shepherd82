How to Calculate High School GPA: A Step-by-Step Guide

Understand grade points, credit weighting, semester GPA, and the difference between weighted and unweighted high school GPA.

Grade point average (GPA) summarizes course grades as a number. Schools can use different grading scales, so the most accurate calculation starts with your school's official grading policy or transcript. The familiar 4.0 scale is common in the United States, but it is not universal.

Understand the grading scale

On a common unweighted 4.0 scale, an A may equal 4.0, a B 3.0, a C 2.0, a D 1.0, and an F 0.0. Some schools use plus/minus values such as A− = 3.7 or B+ = 3.3. Use the values published by your school instead of assuming this exact scale applies.

Calculate an unweighted GPA

Convert each course grade into grade points, add those points, and divide by the number of courses if all courses count equally. Example: grade points of 4.0, 3.0, 3.0, and 2.0 total 12. Divide 12 by 4 courses to get a 3.0 GPA.

Include course credits when needed

If courses have different credit values, calculate a credit-weighted GPA: add (grade points × course credits) for every course, then divide by total credits. For example, a 4-credit course should contribute more than a 1-credit course if your school uses credit weighting.

Weighted vs. unweighted GPA

Some schools add extra points for honors, Advanced Placement, or other advanced classes. This can create a weighted GPA above 4.0. Policies differ significantly, so do not compare weighted GPAs across schools without checking their scales.

Semester GPA and cumulative GPA

A semester GPA covers classes taken during one term. Cumulative GPA combines the relevant courses across multiple terms. To combine semesters accurately, use grade points and credits from all included courses rather than simply averaging semester GPAs when their credit totals differ.

Keep your estimate aligned with your transcript

Check whether your school excludes pass/fail classes, repeats, withdrawn courses, or certain electives. A personal calculator is useful for planning, but the official transcript and school counselor determine the GPA used for admissions or graduation.

Frequently asked questions

Is a 4.0 GPA always the maximum?

No. Four is the maximum on many unweighted scales, but weighted systems can use higher values.

Should I average letter grades directly?

No. Convert grades to the grade-point values defined by your school first.
