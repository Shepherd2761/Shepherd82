Sample Mean: Formula, Example, and When to Use It

Learn the sample mean formula, calculate an arithmetic average from a data set, and understand what the result does and does not tell you.

The sample mean is the arithmetic average of observations collected from a sample. It is often used in statistics to summarize a set of measurements and estimate a population average. The mean is useful, but it can be strongly affected by unusually large or small values.

Sample mean formula

Add all observed values and divide by the number of observations. In notation, x̄ = (x₁ + x₂ + … + xₙ) / n, where x̄ is the sample mean and n is the number of observations. Make sure every value uses the same unit.

Worked example

Suppose five quiz scores are 72, 80, 85, 90, and 93. Their sum is 420. Divide by the five observations: 420 / 5 = 84. The sample mean score is 84.

How to calculate it correctly

List the observations, count how many there are, add the values, and divide the total by the count. For decimal measurements, keep sufficient precision during the calculation and round only the final result. If the data includes a missing value, do not silently treat it as zero unless zero is genuinely the observation.

Mean vs. median

The mean uses every value and is sensitive to outliers. The median is the middle value after sorting the data. For example, in 10, 11, 12, 13, and 100, the mean is 29.2 while the median is 12. The median better describes a typical value when an extreme observation skews the average.

Sample mean and population mean

A sample mean summarizes the observations you collected; a population mean describes every member of the population of interest. Researchers often use a sample mean to estimate a population mean, but the quality of that estimate depends on sampling design, sample size, and variability. A large but biased sample can still produce a misleading estimate.

When not to use a simple mean

A simple mean is not always appropriate for rates, percentages, or groups of different sizes. For example, combining two class percentages may require weighting each class by the number of students. Consider the data-generating process before choosing an average.

Frequently asked questions

Can a sample mean be a decimal?

Yes. The mean can fall between observed values because it is calculated from their total.

Does a larger sample guarantee an accurate mean?

No. Larger random samples often reduce sampling variability, but bias or poor measurement can still lead to inaccurate results.
